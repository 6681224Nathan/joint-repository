2. Theory multiple choice (new questions, quiz 1 style)

  1. In an n-type semiconductor the majority carriers are (a) holes (b) electrons (c) positive ions (d) negative ions.
    Answer(b) electrons. A pentavalent (5-valence-electron) dopant such as phosphorus gives a spare electron.
  2. Pure (intrinsic) silicon at room temperature (a) is a perfect insulator (b) conducts a little because heat frees a few electron-hole pairs (c) conducts like copper.
    Answer(b). At 0 K it is an insulator, because no electron has the energy to reach the conduction band.
  3. A p-type material is made by adding an impurity with (a) 3 (b) 4 (c) 5 valence electrons.
    Answer(a) trivalent, e.g. boron. Each one leaves a hole.
  4. The depletion region contains (a) free electrons only (b) holes only (c) fixed ions and almost no free carriers.
    Answer(c).
  5. The barrier potential of a silicon PN junction is about (a) 0.3 V (b) 0.7 V (c) 1.5 V.
    Answer(b) 0.7 V (germanium about 0.3 V).
  6. Reverse biasing a PN junction makes the depletion region (a) wider (b) narrower (c) unchanged.
    Answer(a) wider, and almost no current flows.
  7. In the ideal diode model, a forward-biased diode is (a) an open circuit (b) a short circuit (c) a 0.7 V source.
    Answer(b) a plain wire. (c) is the constant-voltage-drop model.
  8. The ripple frequency of a full-wave rectifier on 50 Hz mains is (a) 25 Hz (b) 50 Hz (c) 100 Hz.
    Answer(c) 2f.
  9. With ideal diodes, the PIV of each diode in a center-tapped rectifier is (a) Vm (b) 2Vm (c) Vm/2.
    Answer(b) 2Vm.
  10. Making the filter capacitor bigger (a) increases the ripple (b) decreases the ripple (c) changes the ripple frequency.
    Answer(b): Vr = Vm/(frRLC).
  11. The base of a BJT is (a) thick and heavily doped (b) thin and lightly doped (c) the widest region.
    Answer(b). That's why most electrons pass straight through to the collector.
  12. A BJT used as an open switch is in (a) cutoff (b) active (c) saturation.
    Answer(a) cutoff.
  13. For an NPN in saturation, (a) VC > VB (b) VB > VC (c) VBE < 0.5 V.
    Answer(b): both junctions are forward biased.
  14. In a CE amplifier, increasing the collector resistor RC (with no load) makes |Av| (a) larger (b) smaller (c) unchanged.
    Answer(a): Av = −gmRC. (It also moves the Q-point, so not without limit.)
  15. The hybrid-pi resistance rπ equals (a) gm/β (b) β/gm (c) β·gm.
    Answer(b) β/gm = βVT/IC.
  16. Adding a source resistance Rsig in front of a CE amplifier makes the overall gain vo/vsig (a) larger (b) smaller (c) unchanged.
    Answer(b): only Zi/(Zi + Rsig) of vsig reaches the base.

Practice D1 · Dual-supply CE amplifier (quiz 1 style, new numbers) · 20 min

β = 120, VBE(on) = 0.7 V, VT = 0.026 V, ro = ∞, RE fully bypassed by CE. (a) DC analysis: find IB, IC and VCE, and show the transistor is active. (b) Find gm and rπ. (c) Draw the small-signal (hybrid-pi) equivalent circuit. (d) Find the overall gain Av = vo/vsig.

Solution D1

(a) DC (capacitors open, vsig off):

VTH = −10 + 20 × 33/80 = −10 + 8.25 = −1.75 V; RTH = 47 k∥33 k = 19.4 kΩ.

Base loop from VTH down to −10 V: −1.75 − IB(19.4 k) − 0.7 − 121·IB(5.6 k) = −10 → IB = 7.55/(19.4 k + 677.6 k) = 10.8 µA.

IC = 120 × 10.8 µA = 1.30 mA; IE = 1.31 mA. VE = −10 + 1.31 mA × 5.6 k = −2.66 V; VB = −1.96 V; VC = 10 − 1.30 mA × 4.7 k = 3.89 V.

VCE = 3.89 − (−2.66) = 6.55 V > 0.2 V, and VC > VB > VE → active ✓

(b) gm = 1.30 mA/26 mV = 50 mS; rπ = βVT/IC = 120/0.05 = 2.4 kΩ.

(c) Both supplies → ground, all capacitors → wires, the emitter is grounded by CE:

(d) Zi = R1∥R2∥rπ = 19.4 k∥2.4 k = 2.14 kΩ.

vπ = vsig · Zi/(Rsig + Zi) = vsig × 2.14/2.74 = 0.781 vsig.

vo = −gmvπ(RC∥RL) with RC∥RL = 4.7 k∥8.2 k = 2.99 kΩ.

Av = vo/vsig = −0.05 × 2988 × 0.781 = −117 (from base to output alone: −149).

4. Rectifier design practice (quiz 1 style, ideal diodes)

Draw the circuit yourself first (checklist in S2 practice), then compute.

R1 · Center-tapped design · 12 min

Input 220 V RMS, 50 Hz. Design a center-tapped full-wave rectifier with a capacitor filter that gives VDC = 12 V with a ripple Vr = 0.4 V into RL = 200 Ω. (a) Draw the circuit. (b) Vm of each half of the secondary. (c) Ripple frequency. (d) C. (e) Turns ratio (primary peak : whole secondary peak). (f) PIV. (g) Ripple factor.

Solution R1

(a) Center-tapped transformer; each end goes to its own diode, the cathodes join at J; C and RL in parallel from J to the center tap (see S2 section 4).

(b) VDC = Vm − Vr/2 → Vm = 12 + 0.2 = 12.2 V.

(c) Full-wave: fr = 2 × 50 = 100 Hz.

(d) C = Vm/(Vr fr RL) = 12.2/(0.4 × 100 × 200) = 1.53 mF.

(e) Primary peak = 220√2 = 311 V. The whole secondary peaks at 2 × 12.2 = 24.4 V, so 311 : 24.4 = 12.75 : 1 (a 12.2-0-12.2 V peak center-tapped secondary).

(f) PIV = 2Vm = 24.4 V.

(g) r = Vr/VDC = 0.4/12 = 0.033 (3.3%).

R2 · Half-wave from a power rating · 10 min

A device rated 0.9 W at 9 V is powered from 120 V RMS, 60 Hz by a half-wave rectifier with a filter capacitor. Take Vr = 10% of Vm. (a) Draw it. (b) RL. (c) Vm. (d) C. (e) Turns ratio. (f) PIV.

Solution R2

(a) Transformer, one diode in series, C in parallel with RL.

(b) RL = V²/P = 81/0.9 = 90 Ω.

(c) 9 = Vm − 0.05Vm = 0.95Vm → Vm = 9.47 V.

(d) Half-wave: fr = 60 Hz. 0.1 = 1/(60 × 90 × C) → C = 1.85 mF.

(e) 120√2 = 169.7 V, so 169.7 : 9.47 = 17.9 : 1.

(f) Half-wave with a capacitor: PIV = 2Vm = 18.9 V.
