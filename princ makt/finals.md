### Guideline

- competitive landscape
- key ==consumer==. And ==behaviors== and preference
- ==Pricing, distribution==, and ==promotional== strategy

### Segmentation
- Demographics
	- Practical attributes of people
		- Age, gender, income, marital status, and ethnic background
	- ==who they are==
- Psychographics
	- **Personality** and attitudes
		- Activities, attitudes, personality and values
	- ==how they live their life==
- Behavioral
	- **How the product is used, used for what**
		- Benefits, usage rate, patterns
	- ==how they use products==
- Geographics
	- areas where customers live, **area in terms of customer segment, how large is our customer segment**
		- Local, national, international, regional
	- ==where they are==

> STP is segmentation, targeting, and positioning. 4P is product price place promotion

### Customer segments
After segmentation, we figure out the behavioral factors of each segment, to figure out ==what do they want?==

### Evaluating market segment
- market attractiveness factors
	- Customer needs and behavior
		- Are there ==unmet/underserved needs== we can ==satisfy==?
	- Market, segment size, growth rate
		- Market potential in ==units, revenue==, ==number== of potential ==customers==
		- ==Growth== rate
	- Macro environment, ==are they favorable?==
		- External factors : Sociocultural, economic, political, technological, environmental
- Competitive-position factors
	- opportunity for competitive advantage
		- can we differentiate?
		- stage of competing product in product life cycle
	- Firm and competitor and capabilities and resource
		- Management strength
		- Financial resources
		- brand image
		- relative market share
	- attractiveness of industry we would compete
		- Threat of new entrants/substitutes, buyer, power, supplier power...
**Evaluating market segment**
- Market attractiveness factors
	- Is there a pain point we can solve?
	- Is market potential strong enough? Worth it?
	- External factors, do they help us or get in the way?
- Competitive-position factors
	- If we get in, can we be unique?
	- Is our firm strong enough in terms of resources to compete? 
		- Is our brand image strong enough? 
		- Market share as well, what is our market share?
	- Is the industry we would compete in worth it? Does it make money? Enter fees, and cost and size of the industry, from small time application to a barn business 

==we evaluate these factors based on segments==

- size, growth, profit margin
- competition, strategy to enter, resource needed, capability (expertise, and capable of entering, are we good enough?)

### Mass marketing
- For
	- Convenience, and economical ==economy of scale==
- Against
	- Inefficient, ==expensive promotional cost== trying to reach everyone
	- Ineffective
		- ==harder to satisfy everyone==, cant be all things to people
### Differentiated marketing
- For
	- remain competitive
	- ==various offerings to various segments== would give good ==customer satisfaction==
	- response to change in the market, ==product in changed segment, changed, not entire company==
- Against
	- ==High cost== for specialized product
### Niche marketing
For
- Small firm with limited resource (==can focus only on one segment)==
- ==Small market, means less competitor==
- Niche customers stay ==loyal== and willing to pay higher, because we serve them well
- ==strong market position ==and reputation
Against
- ==Slow growth==
- ==low barrier to entry==
- ==brand image got stuck to that segment==, limits marketing potential

### Micromarketing
For
- ==market effectively== in regional and local
- Build ==relationship with customer==
- Support technology available for specific targets
Against
- ==Loose economy of scale==
- ==Vague brand image ==because try to do ==many areas== and be too different

### Price perception
==Value = perceived benefit - perceived cost==
==is it even worth it==?
- effective price setting is understading how much customer values our product and set the right price
- price vs reference price
	- past price
	- cost : raw materials, advertisement, rent
	- purchase context : distribution
### Factors which affect price decision
- Internal
	- ==marketing objective==, what do we want, luxury or pull-rope
		- Market share, volume, profit, prestige
	- marketing strategy
		- STP, mk mix
	- production cost
	- organizational factor
- External
	- consumer
		- customer's value, market size, and price sensitivity
	- competition
		- competition's pricing
	- Economic and regulatory factor ==law==

### select an approximate price level using 3C method
"customer, competition, cost"
- customer : perception of value, 
	- price ceiling : no demand above this price
- competitor : set price based on competitors' strategies, costs, prices, and market offerings
- cost : production cost, no price below this

### Distribution channel functions
- transactional function
	- help buy wholesale, resell, take risk of stock stuck ==moving money from customers to firm==
- logistical function
	- Assorting : find products from many sources to serve customer
	- Storing
	- Sorting : purchase products in large quantity and break it down into smaller amounts desired by customers
	- Transport : physically move products to customer
- Facilitating function
	- financing : UFund
	- grading : inspect test and assign products based on quality
	- marketing information : providing into to customers and supplier, including competitive condition and trends

### Identify major channel alternaties
- number of levels
	- direct vs indirect channel (short, long)
	- dual/multi distribution
- Type of intermediaries
	- types of wholesaler (outlet, merchant, afent), retailer (store, sales force, and online/mobile/machines)
- Number of intermediaries (intensity at each level)
	- Intensive distribution
	- Exclusive distribution
	- Selective distribution

### Distribution, advantages and disadvantages
- direct
	- Pros
	- High quality contact
	- control marketing mi (price promotion, service, quality)
	- Cons
	- ==Smaller coverage==, take longer time to penetrate
	- has fixed cost
	- limited solution
- Indirect channel
	- Pros
	- Large coverage
	- quick penetration time
	- Utilize channel's expertise and resources and knowledge
	- cons
	- High cost, smaller margin
	- ==hard to control ==channel
	- ==reliant on channels==
### When to use what distribution channels?
- direct
	- ==Identifiable customer==
	- product requires ==extensive demonstration==
	- wide ==variety of offerings ==for the target market
	- ==Sufficient resources ==available
	- ==Intermediaries not available== or not capable
- Indirect
	- ==Large no. of customers==
	- ==hard tor reach ==customers
	- product serve market in ==different areas==
	- product used ==heavily, frequently== 
	- ==insufficient resources==
	- ==intermediaries ==perform more ==efficiently== and ==cheaply==

### Levels of intermediary
- 1-level
	- Firm -> retail -> consumer
	- ==durables goods, electronics==, insurance
	- Pros : manufacturers cover ==wide area ==while retaining ==control==
- 2-level
	- Firm -> wholesale -> retail -> consumer
		- ==fast moving consumer goods (FMCG)==
		- markets spread in large area
		- works best if ==retailers== are ==independent ==from each other
	- value of individual purchase is small
	- frequency of purchase is high
- 3-level
	- firm -> ==agent -> wholsaler ==-> retailer ->  consumer
		- Sold by large company
		- ==Wholesalers scattered ==across the country
		- FMCG, garment, durable industry

### Dual distribution and multi-channel
- Dual : direct + indirect
- Multi : use ==various channels simultaneously== to reach firm's target market
- Objectives
	- To ==maximize firm's coverage ==in marketplace
	- Increase ==cost-effectiveness ==of firm's ==marketing effort==
- Occasion
	- Multi-brand
	- Geographical factor
	- Large and small retailer
	- customers in different segment
- Consideration
	- The channel provides ==incremental sales== compared to existing sales

### Advantages and disadvantages
- Pros
	- Reach more and different kinds of customers. ==Reach diverse customers==
	- Tailor ==offerings== to the specific needs of ==diverse segment==
	==the more channels, the more loyal customers==
- Cons
	- ==Hard== to mange and ==control==
		- Price quality promotion

### Communication channel
- objectives
	- increase ==brand awareness==
	- enhance brand ==image==, ==interest==
	- ==stimulate purchase==, repurchase, referral
- "Objectives may be set to move buyers through adoption process"
- Information : awareness, interest -> attention
- Persuading : evaluation, trial -> desire
- Reminding : decision, confirmation -> action

### Communication : target audience
- primary audience : group we want to reach
- secondary audience : may not be interested in product, but can ==influence primary audience==
"target audience affects what will be said, how, when, where it will be said, and who will say"

### Communication : Designing a message
- We need a clear impactful "big idea" ==message==, memorable
- message has to be
	- creative
	- big
	- memorable
	- differentiated to ==stand out== generate attention
- big idea has 3 elements
	- Insight about problem/motivation
	- brand connection
	- key message
![[Pasted image 20260713121556.png]]

### Communication : uncover an insight
- research target audience and product category to find truth about
	- customer's ==pain point==
	- ==motivation forces ==behind one's thoughts/behaviors
- Example
	- Electric razor is less masculine
	- Diet coke is for woman
	- high school students wearing makeup as a form of investment
	- "When you are hungry, it's hard to concentrate and hard to focus on what matters. You don't perform to the level you normally do because there's something missiing"
### Communication : find brand connection
- insight (==the though behind it, deep==)enables a brand to come up with ==solutions/product== to ==challenge ==consumer's ==pain point==
- List ==unique features== and benefits of products that are ==relevant to the insight== that ==competitors cannot== imitate
- "snicker is the perfect way to get back to being yourself. Packed full of peanuts, caramel, nougat, and milk chocolate. Snickers satisfies your hunger - all in a quick and easy-to-carry snack"

### Communication : articulate the idea
- call-to-action ==needs a hook==
- one setence
- find a way to phrase the sentence to hook or grab attention
- "You're not you when you're hungry"
- "There is a reason why Johnnie Walks, don't drink and drive"

### Communication : channel
- To get story to target audience, it's essential to figure out how to get them to interact with you
- **touch point** where, when, and how story is shared
- ==how can you share the message with maximum exposure?==
