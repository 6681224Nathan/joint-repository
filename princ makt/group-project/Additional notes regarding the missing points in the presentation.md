## Target group
- Age : 19-23 (specifically, first to third year MU students, Bachelor's degree)
- Gender : all gender

Briefly, we segment the market based on psychographic factors, or simply put, the lifestyle. There are mainly four lifestyles that we target, those who we think would be satisfied with our product.

From our research, many people fall into the following categorization.
### Main target group, freshmen
- **Freshmen** who just entered the university, who are ==in the stage of forming friend groups== and want to find people to just play sports together, since they still don't know much people, gathering friends to play sports together is almost an impossible task. 
- **Freshmen** who are interested in making new friends, apart from using the campus’s events. 

## Secondary target group, Sophomore, Junior, Senior
 - S**ophomore, junior, senior students** who want to play sports, ==whose friend groups keep skipping== and ditching sport appointment. 
	 - "It's frustrating, sometimes, all I need is to play a match of soccer, simple as that, but my friends, one of them, always randomly have business to tend to. It's impossible to organize a match"
 - **Sophomore, junior, senior students** who want to ==get to know students from other major.== Since the social structure of Mahidol University is found to be separated based on majors, it's hard to constantly gather people from all over the university, from multiple majors to be together and make friends. Sports sound like an answer to many 2nd - 4th year students, but it always comes to a halt when a question arises.
	 - =="Then how can we get people from other major to play sports with us? Other than sports day."==

Thus, we can establish a pain point like this.

## Surface pain point
=="MU students, especially freshmen, struggle to find people to play sports with, or struggle to use sports to make friends from other major”==

## Deeper pain point
Mentioned surface pain points serves to be just a picture to ==clearly illustrate the "practical problem",== but the ==real pain points hides deeper.==

From what we interviewed, we also found out that many students, our target group, the reason why they are **in need** of sports **but can't play** now, is a manifestation of a failure. The failure of MU social structure. 

Many students report that have problems with establishing new connections with friends from university.

"Close friends? Yes, I do have one. But to compare with those I had when I was in Mattayom (high school), I just don't feel as close as before. I don't know why."

Many students also report that the number of people they get to really talk with, that they feel they can get along, is lower than when they were still in high school.

"Man, back then, I was close with my entire classroom, now I can barely get close to anyone"

==It's an irony, university is almost twice the size of high school but it distances people from each other.==

The reason we found is that MU social structure is made to separate people from each other. It gives too much freedom, and that freedom comes with a cost.

## Separation, from classes to buildings, all day to half a day. 

Back when we were in high school, classrooms were close to each other, we can practically walk to other classes by just a couple steps. Even with different studying path (สายการเรียน) it's still possible to walk to your friend. Canteen is united, there are at most 2 soccer field, shared facility forced people to see each other all the time, 8 hours a day, from morning to sunset and we become close because of that. 

But now, there are no classes anymore, we are separated from students of other majors by buildings, each building has its own canteen, their own sport facility even (small one, like Petong field in Engineering building). Each building is separated by a road, and in another building, it is populated by strangers. Class is accounted for maximum 6 hours a day, and each class has different people, different room, different feeling, different vibe (core and general education course), many don't require attendance, less reason to stay at university, more to stay at home. Even worse, the registration system spreads people out from each other, everyone has their own studying plan, some get F, many cannot enroll because the course's seat is full, friends cannot join the same class because of that. With all this, we spend less and less time at shared facility, people become more strangers, there is no "sense of familiarity". We are separated.

## Loneliness
As mentioned, because we get to see less people, get to talk with less varied group of people, spend less time talking to people per day (as mentioned, school forces people to study all day, morning to evening, and eat together, university doesn't), we become lonely and many don't notice that. That's why ==it is not mentioned in the surface pain point== because we don't know that we are lonely, but we are. Even so, there are those that know, and want to solve by playing sports with people, ==finding a way to interact with other people, other than class and occasional event==. that's why we have come up with "Drop In", we get an ideas from these people that figure that sports is a way out of loneliness, we then decide to make that easier and to overcome the barrier of the great question of "how can I play soccer with strangers (other MU students) that we don't know before".  

**We intend to cure the loneliness by bring sports to people, we make them spend more time together, to have something fun and engaging to do with each other, that they can do every week, or even everyday. Constant, fun. Joy**
